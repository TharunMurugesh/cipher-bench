# CipherBench Novelty Review

**Review date:** 2026-10-23  
**Scope:** shallow 8×8 digits CNN; square activation; training-time penalty on convolution pre-activations; pixel scaling; CKKS scale/level feasibility; packed/im2col convolution. This is a focused scoping review, not a systematic review or a claim of priority.

## Focused search and novelty judgment

Search performed on 2026-10-23 using primary-source-oriented searches for: `homomorphic encryption CKKS activation range regularization square activation CNN pixel scaling`; `CKKS convolution square activation range regularization neural network paper`; and `site:arxiv.org homomorphic encryption range regularization CKKS convolution`. The search identified prior range-control/training work and recent overflow-range work above. It did not establish whether the exact conjunction of (a) pixel /16 on sklearn digits 0/1 and 3/8, (b) squared-hinge regularization for a one-kernel square-activation model, (c) split-controlled ablations and (d) mask-vs-im2col diagnostics has been studied elsewhere. This scope is too narrow to assert that no exact match exists.

**Assessment:** the Phase 3 candidate tests a useful experimental combination, but its ingredients have clear prior art: polynomial/square HE CNNs, activation-range control, CKKS parameter calibration, and packed/im2col convolution. The most defensible near-term contribution is a reproducible, narrowly scoped comparison with explicit leakage controls and numerical/cost diagnostics. Architectural novelty is **provisional and unverified**. The range-penalty hypothesis was not demonstrated: the selected A3 bound was violated by 76.3% of primary and 85.0% of secondary test pre-activations, and A3 tied pixel-only A1 on both test accuracies. The `im2col` path decrypts before pooling and is not a privacy-equivalent optimization.

## Final evidence update

The final report confirms the same conservative conclusion. On the digits 0-vs-1 and 3-vs-8 saved test splits, A1 (pixel scaling) and A3 (pixel scaling plus the training-only range penalty) have identical predictions and tie in accuracy. On the primary task, all A0–A3 have 99.07% accuracy. On the secondary task, A1/A3 reach 99.07% while A0/A2 reach 95.37%; this is one outer split per task, not statistical evidence across splits. Three fixed-row seed repetitions are validation runs only.

The Phase 2 held-out CKKS path matched the float64 graph's class decisions 108/108, while Phase 3 CKKS timings and size measurements were obtained on 38 primary validation rows. Those results show implementation-level numeric fidelity under a specific exploratory chain; they do not establish full encrypted classification. The head is plaintext after pooled-feature decryption, and the im2col path decrypts before pooling.

**Final novelty statement:** CipherBench contributes a transparent reconstruction and artifact-backed diagnostic study. It does not claim that RC-TinyCNN is unprecedented, that range regularization improves accuracy beyond pixel scaling, or that the measured local CKKS accuracy reproduces or exceeds the paper's result.

The study must not be described as fully encrypted end-to-end inference: encrypted convolution and square were tested, but the pooling/head boundary is plaintext in the `im2col` route, and the mask route decrypts pooled features before its linear head. No formal security level, overflow proof, or certified numerical-error guarantee is claimed.
